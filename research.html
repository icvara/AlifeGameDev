ALife and video games in research

Artificial life and video games have fed into each other for decades. Some research uses ALife to make games (evolving creatures, adaptive enemies, generated content), and some uses games as a playground to study open-ended, lifelike systems.

A few landmark papers:

  • 1994 Evolving Virtual Creatures Karl Sims · SIGGRAPH '94 Bodies and neural "brains" evolved together by a genetic algorithm in a physics simulation, producing creatures that swim, walk and jump. A big inspiration for creature games that followed.
  • 1997 Creatures: Artificial Life Autonomous Software Agents for Home Entertainment Stephen Grand, Dave Cliff, Anil Malhotra · Autonomous Agents '97 The technical story behind Creatures: Norns with neural networks, an artificial biochemistry with hormone-like signals, and genetics, sold as a commercial game.
  • 2005 Real-Time Neuroevolution in the NERO Video Game Kenneth O. Stanley, Bobby D. Bryant, Risto Miikkulainen · IEEE Trans. on Evolutionary Computation rtNEAT evolves neural networks while the game is running. Players train a squad of robots whose behaviour visibly improves as they play.
  • 2009 Evolving Content in the Galactic Arms Race Video Game Erin J. Hastings, Ratan K. Guha, Kenneth O. Stanley · IEEE CIG 2009 Weapons evolve based on what players actually use, so the game keeps generating new content shaped by its community.
  • 2017 Neuroevolution in Games: State of the Art and Open Challenges Sebastian Risi, Julian Togelius · IEEE Trans. on Computational Intelligence and AI in Games A survey of how evolved neural networks are used in games, from controlling agents to generating content, and of the open problems.
  • 2021 Video Games as a Testbed for Open-Ended Phenomena Sam Earle, Julian Togelius, Lisa B. Soros · IEEE Conference on Games 2021 Flips the direction: uses an interactive Game of Life and SimCity to study open-endedness, one of ALife's big open questions.
  • 2023 Automata Quest: NCAs as a Video Game Life Mechanic Hiroki Sato, Tanner Lund, Takahide Yoshida, Atsushi Masumori · ALIFE 2023 workshop Asks why "life" in games is still mostly a health bar, and proposes neural cellular automata as a living alternative.

The ALIFE conference also hosts a workshop on exactly this topic: ALife for and from Video Games. Know a paper that belongs here? Suggest it in the survey.
