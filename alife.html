introduction

What is artificial life?

Artificial life (ALife) is the study of life by trying to build it. You don't describe how living things behave. You set up simple rules and see what lifelike behaviour comes out on its own: movement, cooperation, competition, adaptation, evolution.

The field is usually split three ways: soft ALife (simulations in software), hard ALife (robots), and wet ALife (synthetic biology in the lab). Games live firmly on the soft side.

In a game, this means handing part of the design over to the simulation: creatures with their own needs and brains, species that change between play sessions, ecosystems that keep going when you look away. Things happen that nobody scripted, and that's a large part of the appeal.

Evolving brains

Fig. 1 runs on fixed rules. A step further is to let the rules themselves evolve. Two tools show up in many ALife games: neural networks, small artificial brains that turn what a creature senses into what it does, and genetic algorithms, which copy the best performers with small random mutations, generation after generation.

Ecosystems

Put several species in the same world and you get an ecosystem: feedback loops where every population depends on the others. More grass feeds more herbivores, more herbivores feed more predators, and too many predators empty the world of herbivores, until the predators starve and the cycle starts again.
